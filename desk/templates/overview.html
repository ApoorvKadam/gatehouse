Where it differs

The agent's work lands on the books, with its rule beside it.

In the storage FMS and AI products we surveyed from their public pages (November 2026), a shipped AI front desk hands a fee question to staff as a ticket, or is not allowed to change tenant data. Approval steps and dollar limits for AI appear only in demos and stated plans. None shows the rule an AI applied beside the books entry it wrote.

Gatehouse is the FMS, so Porter's work is settled on the facility's own books. A fee posted twice is reversed, a fee posted in error is removed, and a dispute pauses collection. Each one shows what Porter read, what the books showed, the rule that allowed it and the entries it wrote.

One message, end to end

“You charged me twice for the November late fee.”

  1. Porter reads itOne claim: the November late fee appears twice. It quotes the tenant's own words and names no amount it did not see.
  2. Code checks the booksTwo late fees for November rent on unit B238: one posted by billing on Nov 6, one posted again by hand on Nov 10.
  3. The gate actsThe second copy is reversed in one balanced entry: fee income down $20.00, owed by the tenant down $20.00. A second press changes nothing.
  4. Code writes the reply“You are right: the $20.00 late fee was posted twice for the same thing. The second one has been reversed.”

When it cannot finish

It stops, says why, and puts the work in front of a person.

A card charged twice

Faye says her card was charged twice on November 2. The books do not hold the card processor's records, so Porter hands it to staff with that reason. Nothing on her account changes.

A fee she never signed for

Priya disputes a $25 admin fee. It is not in the schedule she signed, so the dispute is recorded and collection pauses. The office manager removes the fee or keeps it under review, on Today.
